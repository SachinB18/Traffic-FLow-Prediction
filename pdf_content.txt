II. RELATED WORK 
In this section, we review various deep learning methods that 
have contributed to traffic prediction. These methods can be 
roughly divided into two categories, one is the traditional deep 
learning methods that mainly use CNNs, RNNs, and GCNs, and 
the other is the deep neural networks that combine attention 
mechanisms. 
Traditional deep learning methods. As a typical study using 
CNN for traffic forecasting, in [10], the researchers creatively 
adopted the branch network method to model the closeness, 
period, and trend of traffic data, which made the feature 
modeling process clearer and opened up ideas for the later ones. 
Dai et al. [14] proposed an ST-InNet to model the spatio-
temporal correlations and the difference in the contributions of 
spatio-temporal correlations. This method used the Inception 
network and feature fusion to model traffic characteristics, which 
improved the efficiency of traffic prediction. In the same way, in 
[15], 3D convolution was introduced into ST-ResNet and 
designed a ΓÇ£RecalibrationΓÇ¥ to model the dynamic spatio-
temporal correlations.  
On the other hand, RNNs are more concerned with modeling 
temporal-dependent relationships. Long short-term memory 
(LSTM) [2] network is widely used in traffic prediction, where 
the network models spatio-temporal correlation through multiple 
memory units. In addition, in [16], the researchers proposed a 
diffusion convolutional recurrent network (DCRNN), which 
captures the spatial dependency using bidirectional random 
walks on the graph, and the temporal dependency using the 
encoder-decoder architecture with scheduled sampling. 
Besides, based on the computational properties of GCN, this 
structure is also widely used in the field of traffic prediction. Yu 
et al. [17] proposed a spatio-temporal graph convolutional neural 
network (STGCN). This method applies graph convolutional 
neural networks to traffic prediction for the first time, in which 
Temporal Gated-Conv is used to capture temporal associations 
and Spatial Graph-Conv is used to capture spatial associations, 
and achieves good results. Meanwhile, multiple modules with 
different periods are designed in the model to effectively capture 
the heterogeneity in the local spatiotemporal graph. Bai et al. [18] 
proposed an adaptive graph convolutional neural network 
(AGCRN) for traffic prediction, which uses node adaptive 
parameter learning and data-adaptive graph generation modules 
to model spatio-temporal dependencies, outperforming current 
state-of-the-art techniques in the absence of a predefined graph 
structure about spatial connectivity. 
In summary, predictive models based on CNN structures 
performed well in modeling spatial dependencies, but they were 
deficient in modeling temporal dependencies. RNN-based 
prediction models perform excellently in modeling temporal 
dependencies, but their modeling of spatial dependencies is 
flawed. The GCN-based prediction model can fully exploit the 
spatial dependencies between road nodes, but this network 
structure is difficult to train and difficult to handle raster data. 
The hybrid network of the three computational modes can make 
up for their respective shortcomings, but it greatly increases the 
complexity of the network as well as the difficulty of training. 
Attention-based prediction methods. With the great success 
of Transformer in the field of natural language processing, many 
researchers have studied the application of this idea to the field 
of traffic prediction. Shi et al. [13] proposed an attention-based 
periodic temporal neural network (APTN), which combines the 
self-attention mechanism and LSTM, to predict future traffic 
flow. Tang et al. [19] proposed a general spatio-temporal graph 
attention-based dynamic graph convolutional network (GAGCN) 
model to predict traffic flow. The method proved the predictive 
performance of its network on two datasets by combining the 
attention mechanism and graph convolutional computation. Guo 
et al. [20] constructed a prediction model by combining attention 
with graph convolution. The model uses graph convolution to 
model spatial features, convolution to model temporal features 
as well as dynamic spatio-temporal correlations through a spatio-
temporal attention mechanism. This approach cannot efficiently 
model long-range time dependencies.  
STFormer learns the strong points of the above methods, 
mainly utilizes multi-head attention to model dynamic spatio-
temporal dependencies, introduces the residual connections to 
improve the model convergence speed, and enhances the 
expressive power of the model based on parameter matrices.

The standard vision transformer obtains a sequence by 
reshaping the patches of images. Similarly, we performed a 
similar process for the traffic flow matrix. The ViT uses 
constant latent vector size D through all of its layers, so we 
map each patch to a fixed-length vector at the spatial level 
and at the temporal level, respectively. 
As shown in Fig. 2, the temporal transformer mainly 
consists of the data temporal tokenize layer, temporal 
positional encoding layer, and multi-head attention layer. The 
temporal transformer first serializes the multi-frame traffic 
data, followed by adding temporal position information, and 
then extracts dynamic temporal correlation features through 
multi-head attention. 
Data Temporal Tokenize: The passage of time series is an 
important factor influencing the dynamics of traffic flow. 
This is a linear, constant process of change and it is necessary 
to first characterize each moment of traffic data as a feature 
of that moment, and then tokenize that feature. Based on the 
closeness time, we consider each moment of the flow as a 
token. For the closeness and period dependent sequences with 
lc length and lp length, we transform them into vectors using 
the learnable linear projection. We refer to the output of this 
projection as the temporal patch embeddings of the data. 
Specifically, for a temporal matrix of inputs, which shape

B. Performance Comparison 
Table II shows the RMSE and MAE of the prediction 
results for STFoemer as well as for all baselines on the four 
datasets. Different models have different predictive 
performances on different data, but STFormer all achieved 
the best prediction performance. Specifically, DKFN has the 
worst prediction performance, which is because this method 
does not sufficiently take into account the dynamic 
characteristics of spatio-temporal dependent features when 
modeling them. Compared to the AGCRN and STTN 
networks, which incorporate an attention mechanism, 
Seq2seq, and STResNet have different performances on the 
four data. Different data have different dynamic spatio-
temporal dependencies, and these two approaches show 
shortcomings in modeling such features. Similarly, AGCRN 
and STTN show that they lack robustness and are weak to 
new data. PDFormer, as the current more advanced prediction 
model, has achieved good prediction performance. The traffic 
delay-aware module constructed by this model enhances the 
feature representation ability of the model. Our proposed 
STFormer achieves the best prediction on all four datasets, 
which proves the robustness of the model. More specifically, 
on the BikeNYC dataset, compared to baselines, STFormer 
has an average reduction of 5.25% and 5.71% in RMSE and 
MAE, respectively, and on the T_Drive dataset by an average 
of 14.31% and 20.02%, and on the TaxiNYC dataset by an 
average of 19.46% and 22.67%, and on the BikeCHI dataset 
by an average of 12.74% and 17.19%. 
In summary, STFormer models dynamic spatio-temporal 
dependencies using the multi-head attention mechanism and 
further enhances the STFormerΓÇÖs expressive power by using a 
parameter matrix-based fusion approach, as well as introducing 
residual connections to speed up the convergence of the network.  
STFormer predicts an overall average reduction in RMSE and 
MAE of 12.94% and 14.60% over the four datasets.

V. CONCLUSIONS 
In this paper, we propose a dynamic spatio-temporal 
transformer network (STFormer) for traffic flow prediction 
that learns dynamic spatio-temporal dependencies in traffic 
flow data. We design temporal and spatial transformers for 
modeling dynamic temporal dependencies and dynamic 
spatial dependencies, respectively, by exploiting the 
computational properties of the multi-attention mechanism. 
And the introduction of residual connections improves the 
convergence speed of the model. In addition, the parameter 
matrix-based feature fusion method enhances the feature 
representation 
capability 
of 
STFormer. 
STFormer 
outperforms the state-of-the-art methods in prediction on four 
real, publicly available datasets and demonstrates robustness 
in multi-step prediction. 
In future work, we will further explore the prediction 
performance of the model in traffic graph data, with a view 
to achieving better prediction performance in more types of 
traffic data.
